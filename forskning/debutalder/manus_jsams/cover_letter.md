Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript "Does starting age predict who stays? Competition debut and long-term participation in youth track and field" for consideration as **Original Research** in the sub-discipline **Physical activity and public health (as relevant to sport and exercise)**.

Whether children should enter competitive sport early is contested: position statements on early specialisation warn that intensive training and competition at young ages risk overuse injury and burnout, while the largest prospective dropout study in football found that those who left had started organised football later than those who stayed. No study has followed a complete national entry population of an individual sport and asked what age at entry implies for who is still competing a decade later. Using the Norwegian Athletics Federation's registry, which records every sanctioned result from the first eligible age of 10, this study follows all 17 496 athletes born 2001–2005 whose first registered competition fell at ages 10–16, to October 2026.

The main findings are that senior conversion was U-shaped in debut age with a null linear trend; that measured at equal career length, later starters persisted far less than those who entered at 10; that the first season after entry was the universal filter at every entry age; and that among athletes competing at 17, entry history added little predictive information once current competition volume was known. Early competitive entry did not undermine retention. The study reports descriptive population parameters for federations and clubs and offers no causal claims.

The analysis plan was committed to version control before any exposure–outcome model was estimated. Reporting follows STROBE.

The manuscript has not been published previously, is not under consideration elsewhere, and will not be submitted elsewhere until a decision has been made by the Journal. It is not available on a preprint server. Three other studies of the same registry are cited (a relative-age study in press, a competition-volume cohort study under review, and a performance-progression study under review); they use different exposures and samples, and no text or result overlaps with the present manuscript.

The study analyses competition results registered by the Norwegian Athletics Federation and is not medical or health research under Norway's Health Research Act, so approval by a Regional Committee for Medical and Health Research Ethics was not required. Sikt (the Norwegian Agency for Shared Services in Education and Research) confirmed that the use of the data raises no data-protection concerns. No participants were contacted or recruited, and no individual is identified.
