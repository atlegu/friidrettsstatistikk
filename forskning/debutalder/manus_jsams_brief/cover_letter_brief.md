Dear Professor Meyer,

Thank you for your decision on manuscript JSAMS-D-26-02238, "Does starting age predict who stays? Competition debut and long-term participation in youth track and field", and for suggesting that the work might be suitable as a Brief Report. I resubmit it in that format, in the sub-discipline **Physical activity and public health (as relevant to sport and exercise)**.

The manuscript has been shortened to the Brief Report limits: 1 500 words of main text, one table and one figure, and an abstract of 100 words. The full set of analyses is retained in the supplementary material (Tables S1–S6, Figures S1–S3), including the sample flow, sex-specific conversion, retention with confidence intervals for every cell, the equal-career-length outcomes at five and six years, and the pre-specified robustness analyses. No analysis has been changed; the numbers are those of the original submission.

The study follows all 17 496 athletes born 2001–2005 whose first registered competition fell at ages 10–16 in the Norwegian Athletics Federation's registry. Its three findings are that senior conversion is U-shaped in debut age with a null linear trend; that measured at equal career length, later starters persist far less than those who entered at 10; and that among athletes competing at 17, entry history adds little once current competition volume is known. Early competitive entry carried no retention penalty, and the first season after entry was the decisive filter at every entry age.

The manuscript has not been published previously, is not under consideration elsewhere, and is not available on a preprint server. Three further studies of the same registry are cited in the manuscript: a relative-age study in press (*Sports Medicine – Open*), a competition-volume cohort study under review (*International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching*), and a performance-progression study under review (*Psychology of Sport and Exercise*); they use different exposures and samples, and no text or result overlaps with the present manuscript. A longitudinal performance-development study of the registry is published in this journal (J Sci Med Sport 2026, doi:10.1016/j.jsams.2026.07.006); it concerns performance trajectories, not retention, and is cited in the Introduction.

The study analyses competition results registered by the federation and is not medical or health research under Norway's Health Research Act, so approval by a Regional Committee for Medical and Health Research Ethics was not required. Sikt (the Norwegian Agency for Shared Services in Education and Research) confirmed that the use of the data raises no data-protection concerns. No participants were contacted or recruited, and no individual is identified.
