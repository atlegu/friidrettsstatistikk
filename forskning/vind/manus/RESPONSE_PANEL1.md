# Response to the round-1 panel — "How much is the wind worth?"

All new analyses are in `07_revisjon_r1.py` and `10_eiv_kalibrering.py` (outputs `tables/estimering/r1_*.csv`); every figure and table is generated from those files by `08_r1_figurer_tabeller.py` and `09_r1_supptex.py`, and every number in the text and in this letter is checked against them by an automated checker (`audit/kontroll_tall.py`). An independent reimplementation in R reproduces the analysis sample row for row and all estimates. That pre-submission audit also found and corrected errors of our own, listed under R2-2 and R2-4. The two reworks the editor made preconditions — design-consistent clustering and the H5 recomputation from the tailwind branch — were both done, and the H5 rework changed a headline conclusion: we report that openly below (R2-5).

## Reviewer 1 (biomechanics / sprint aerodynamics)

**R1-1 (mechanism sentence has the wrong sign).** The reviewer is right, and we are grateful for the catch. A growing aerodynamic share at higher speeds predicts a percentage effect *rising* with age; ours falls. The Discussion now says exactly that: "The residual decline cannot be the aerodynamic share, which grows with speed and would push the percentage effect the other way; it is consistent with a fixed wind being a larger fraction of a slower runner's airspeed, and perhaps with children's higher surface-to-mass ratio." On the suggested quasi-physical calibration: we have added the model-based $v^2$ benchmark where it is decisive (the asymmetry, R1-3) but have not re-fitted a Linthorne/Mureika-type model across bands in this revision --- those models are parameterised on elite adults (drag area, stature, power profiles), and extrapolating their parameters to children would add exactly the unvalidated layer the paper is trying to avoid. We flag it as the natural next step and are happy to add it if the editor regards it as a condition.

**R1-2 (bend events; gauge protocol).** Implemented in full. (i) The per-duration comparison is now explicit in Results and in a new Supplementary Table S4: the straight sprints line up at 0.0059--0.0063 s per m/s per second of race time, the bend events at 0.0040--0.0043 --- about a third lower, as the gauge protocol predicts. (ii) Methods now state what a "+1 m/s" reading operationally means per event family (10 s from the gun in the 100 m, 13 s in the short hurdles, 10 s from entering the straight in the 200 m, 5 s per attempt in the jumps). (iii) The same passage states that Norwegian practice applies these conventions to the domestic distances (60/80 m as the 100 m; 150 m as the 200 m). The limitation paragraph now names the bend-gauge issue as partly measurement, not physiology.

**R1-3 ("exactly as drag physics predicts" overstates).** Agreed and reworked. A new Table 4 reports both wind branches with meet-clustered CIs, the observed cost/gain ratios (1.51 / 1.66 / 2.37 on 60/100/200 m) and the $v^2$ benchmark at each event's mean speed (1.14--1.18). The text now reads the asymmetry as directional, not calibrated, and names candidate contributors (selective behaviour dampening the tailwind side; on the 200 m, the straight-only gauge reading). "Exactly as drag physics predicts" is gone everywhere (Results, Discussion, Conclusion). Quadratic terms for all nine events are in Supplementary Table S5.

**R1-4 (lower bound invalid for jumps).** Agreed. The lower-bound claim is now scoped to the sprints in Methods, Robustness, Discussion and Conclusion; the jumps are read as "approximations of uncertain sign" with the upward attempt-selection force stated. On bounding the attenuation: we now report the within-meet share of wind variance per event (25--35 % sprints, 40--53 % jumps) and compute the classical race-level gauge error that would reproduce the observed meet-FE attenuation --- an SD of 0.40--0.58 m/s from 60 to 200 m (see R2-4) --- which quantifies the EIV reading without asserting it.

**Minors.** (1) Elite benchmark harmonised to 0.04--0.06 in both places. (2) Measurement windows per event family now stated (see R1-2). (3) The jumps' absolute (cm) age gradients are Supplementary Table S1, with a sentence in Results: in cm the jump effect *rises* with age (girls' long jump 1.9 → 4.1 cm per m/s) as flight lengthens --- the same exposure logic as the sprints, run in reverse. (4) Done: per second of race time the pooled hurdle codes run 0.0048--0.0069 s per m/s (Table S4), spanning the straight-sprint rates, and this is cited where the elevated 100/110 m estimates are discussed; longer race times account for much, though not all, of the elevation. (5) Kept "to our knowledge" and tempered the uniqueness claim. (6) Error bars (meet-clustered 95 % CIs) added to Figures 3 and 4.

## Reviewer 2 (sports statistics / econometrics)

**R2-1 (monotonicity contradicted; 0.144 vs 0.143).** Both fixed. The claim now reads "declined with age in every sprint and both sexes --- monotonically in every series except the sparse girls' 200 m at 10--11", and the abstract says "declined" without the adverb. 0.143 replaces 0.144 in abstract and text. A full text-vs-files reconciliation pass was run: every table in the revision is generated from the estimation CSVs by script (`08`/`09`), not typed, and the two hand-quoted ranges the reviewer caught were re-derived from the files.

**R2-2 (hurdles text misdescribes the table).** The reviewer was right, and checking it exposed two further errors of ours: the round-1 table omitted one sex-specific row (boys' 100 m hurdles at 84 cm, 709 results), and the flat references used each code's pooled modal age band, which for that code is the senior women's 20--34 although the boys are all 14--15. Table 5 now has all thirteen sex-specific rows, with flat references (and their CIs) from each sex's own modal band. The section is rewritten to match: the 60 m hurdles match the flat 60 m (0.055--0.067 against 0.056--0.070; 21 % below to 9 % above, overlapping intervals), while the 100/110 m hurdles mostly exceed the flat 100 m (4 % below to 48 % above), with the interval entirely above the reference in two youth classes (girls' 76.2 cm, boys' 84 cm). The section title and the abstract now say exactly this instead of "behave like flat sprints".

**R2-3 (clustering level).** Adopted as primary. Wind is constant within a race, so Tables 2--6, the figures and all new supplementary tables now carry meet-clustered 95 % CIs (Moulton 1986 cited in Methods); athlete clustering --- the plan's original choice --- is retained as robustness, with a note that point estimates are identical; the reviewer's concern was well founded, as meet-clustered intervals are 1.5--2.0 times wider in the large sprints (and similar in the jumps). The H4 slope-equality p-values are re-stated under meet clustering (p < 0.001 on 60/100/200 m; Table 4).

**R2-4 (meet-FE attenuation framed one-sidedly).** We did the requested arithmetic (`10_eiv_kalibrering.py`). The within-meet share of wind variance is 25--35 % in the sprints and 40--53 % in the jumps. For each sprint we computed the wind variance that identifies β under each specification and solved for the classical race-level gauge-error SD that makes the ratio of reliabilities equal the observed ratio of estimates: 0.40 m/s (60 m), 0.49 m/s (100 m) and 0.58 m/s (200 m), with reliability falling from 0.92/0.88/0.85 to 0.80/0.71/0.50 --- for the 100 m this reproduces 0.082 → 0.066. The Robustness section gives this in compressed form *and* concedes the observationally equivalent confounding reading; it also now reports the attenuation correctly as 13--41 % for 60/100/200 m (round 1 said 15--40 %) and states that meet effects *raise* the jump estimates by 26--45 % (round 1 said "slightly"), which measurement error alone would not produce. "Bracketing" is retained for the sprints with both readings stated. We kept the abstract on the pre-specified primary estimates, within the journal's word limit, and the Robustness section states the bracket.

**R2-5 (H5 internally inconsistent with H4; denominator under-specified).** Implemented in full, and it mattered. (a) Table 6 (window value) is recomputed from the *tailwind-branch* slope per event × sex × band (Supplementary Table S3 gives all slopes with CIs). (b) The SD denominator is now defined precisely: the pooled within-cell SD of *wind-adjusted* marks over cells with ≥ 3 results --- free of wind-driven variance (no circularity) and of the small-cell bias the reviewer computed (pooling across cells with Bessel correction, rather than averaging per-cell SDs at n = 2, removes the c₄ problem). (c) Consequence, reported openly: the SD-standardised age gradient largely disappears. With honest tailwind slopes and an honest denominator, the +2.0 m/s window is worth 0.2--0.7 SD across events, sexes and ages, with no systematic youth excess (the round-1 "0.9--1.1 SD at 12--13 vs 0.5--0.8 senior" rested on the symmetric slope and a singleton-deflated SD). The fairness argument now rests where it genuinely holds: the window licenses the largest contribution in *seconds* and per second of race time in the youngest classes, which is the unit season lists and records are kept in; the abstract, Results 3.6, Discussion and Conclusion are all rewritten accordingly, and the anomalous 60 m 16--17 cells are flagged as thin.

**R2-6 (ability tertiles defined on the outcome).** Redefined as the athlete-season mean of wind-adjusted performance (each mark corrected to still air with the event-level slope), with the contributing athlete-seasons divided into equal thirds within event × sex × band. The gradient survives: 100 m 0.088 / 0.083 / 0.079 (low/mid/high), 60 m 0.066 / 0.058 / 0.056, 200 m 0.132 / 0.120 / 0.117, jumps not separating; Figure 4 is rebuilt on the new definition with CIs.

**R2-7 (selection and heaping diagnostics).** (a) Reworded --- "mild" is gone; the text now says the 9.5 % exact zeros exceed what a continuous wind distribution would produce and are consistent with some unoperated gauges recorded as calm, and the exclusion robustness is leaned on. (c) Missing wind is characterised: 4.9 % of approved outdoor results lack a reading (18,692 rows at the exclusion step, after the other filters), 9.1 % in the smallest third of meets vs 1.5 % in the largest, and between 2 and 7 % by year; stated in Data and in limitations (completeness, not within-athlete bias). (d) 2020's omission from the period split is now explained (curtailed pandemic season). (e) The estimating n (315,288 in cells ≥ 2) is now in the abstract and Data.
