# Preregistration (draft): Finding your event — search and match within youth track and field

*English draft of 1 November 2026, written from the Norwegian analysis plan (`ANALYSEPLAN.md`, version 1). To be completed and registered on OSF before any exposure–outcome model is estimated. Nothing has been run.*

## 1. Study information

**Title.** Finding your event: search and match within youth track and field — a population register test of why early breadth predicts later performance.

**Authors.** Atle G. Guttormsen (Norwegian University of Life Sciences) and Gøran [surname] (affiliation).

**Background.** Early breadth of sporting activity is associated with later world-class performance (Güllich, Barth, Hambrick & Macnamara, *Science*, 2025). At least five mechanisms can produce the same association: transfer of learning capital, search and match, reduced risk, reverse causation (able children succeed in and continue with more activities), and maturation timing as a common cause. They have not been tested against one another on population data. Search and match makes a prediction the others do not: switches between disciplines should go *towards* the discipline in which the athlete was already relatively best, and the switch should be followed by progression. The Norwegian Athletics Federation registry records every sanctioned result in every event, event groups are clearly delimited, and age-graded points (Tyrving) give a common scale, so the mechanism can be tested within one sport. Three earlier studies of the registry frame the question: within-athlete progression predicts retention (study A1), current engagement dominates history (study A3 and the IJSSC cohort study), and relative-age selection is complete at entry. None asks what progression consists of, or whether athletes find their event.

**Hypotheses.** All concern athletes still competing at ages 15–16 (landmark), so that switches are observed without conditioning on the senior outcome.

- **H1 (descriptive).** A substantial share of athletes at 13–14 are *mismatched*: relatively best (standardized age-graded points) in an event group other than the one they compete most in. No test; shares with CIs by sex and main group.
- **H2 (direction of switching; primary test).** Among mismatched athletes, the probability of switching main event group from 13–14 to 15–16 is higher than among matched athletes, and switches go more often to the athlete's best 13–14 group than expected by chance among the other groups the athlete competed in. Estimands: (a) adjusted OR for switching, mismatched vs. matched; (b) observed minus chance-expected share of switches to the best group, with bootstrap CI. Search and match predicts (a) > 1 and (b) > 0; transfer and reverse causation predict (a) ≈ 1 or (b) ≈ 0.
- **H3 (progression after switching).** Athletes who switch towards their best 13–14 group show larger standardized progression from 13–14 to 15–16 (measured in the new main group) than mismatched non-switchers and matched non-switchers. Estimand: adjusted differences in standardized Δ with CIs. Under reverse causation, switchers have higher level in *all* groups already at 13–14 and the difference disappears when level in the best and second-best group is controlled.
- **H4 (participation breadth vs. ability breadth; secondary).** Among athletes with results in at least two groups at 13–14, participation breadth (number of groups; HHI) predicts senior outcomes; under reverse causation the association disappears when ability breadth (standardized points in the second-best group) and best-group level are added; under transfer it persists. Estimand: change in the breadth coefficient between the two models, with bootstrap CI.

**Pre-stated interpretation rules.** Search and match is supported only if both H2 and H3 hold. H2 without H3 is consistent with athletes choosing what they are already good at without gaining from it; H3 without H2 is tested against regression to the mean. Reverse causation is the preferred reading if switchers are better in all groups at 13–14 and the H3 difference vanishes under control for best and second-best level. Transfer cannot be separated from search and match by H2–H3; H4 is the only test that speaks to transfer and is weak (subsample, definition-dependent). Maturation timing is addressed only descriptively through birth quarter. A null result speaks against search and match *within* athletics, where switching is cheap and the club and coach are often the same; it does not speak to search and match between sports, which the registry cannot see. No causal effect of breadth is estimated.

## 2. Data and what has already been observed

**Data.** The registry extract frozen for study A1 on 29 October 2026 (all results from the calendar year the athlete turned 13, athletes born 1998–2006, after the October 2026 deduplication). Event groups follow the registry's event categories: sprint, jumps, throws, runs of 800 m and longer (middle distance, long distance and steeplechase combined), hurdles. Combined events, relays, race walking and "other" are not used in group definitions. Sex × group cells with fewer than 20 athletes are excluded.

**Already observed before registration (declared).** (i) Study A1 computed, for its primary sample, the main event group at 13–14, the best group at 15–16, and progression measured in each; 29.2 % of A1's primary sample were relatively best at 13–14 in a group other than their main group. (ii) Study A1 reported, as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis, that progression measured in the best 15–16 group predicts senior activity (OR 2.53 per SD in the group-consistent variant; 1.87 in the plan's variant; n = 4 071). This is an outcome association involving switchers, but it is not the search-and-match test: it does not condition on match at 13–14, does not measure the direction of switching, and does not compare switchers with non-switchers. (iii) Coverage counts, sample flow and distributions of match, switching, breadth and progression without any outcome will be computed and reported before registration. **No cross-tabulation of match, switch direction or breadth against senior or progression outcomes has been or will be computed before registration.** A technical guard requires a registration marker file before the H2–H4 scripts execute.

**Sample.** Athletes born 1998–2005 (2006 for switch and progression outcomes only) with ≥1 approved result at 13–14 and ≥1 at 15–16 and known sex (4 909 in A1's flow), with scorable points in at least one group in both windows for H1–H3 and results in at least two groups at 13–14 for H4. Replication cohorts: born 1998–2001 and 2002–2005.

## 3. Variables

**Main group per window:** the group with most results (ties: highest standardized points, then alphabetical). **Best group per window:** the group with the highest standardized mean-of-3-best points among groups with ≥2 scorable results (≥1 as sensitivity). **Match at 13–14:** matched if main group = best group; otherwise mismatched; match gap = standardized points in the best group minus in the main group (sensitivity: mismatched only if gap ≥ 0.5 SD). **Switch:** main group at 15–16 ≠ main group at 13–14; direction: towards the best 13–14 group, towards another group the athlete competed in, or towards a new group; secondary: switching by 17–19 and as a senior. **Progression:** A1's group-consistent Δ (mean of 3 best standardized points at 15–16 minus at 13–14, both in the 15–16 main group), with result counts k13 and k15 as covariates; mean-of-3-best, not the maximum. **Participation breadth at 13–14:** number of groups with ≥1 result; HHI over result shares. **Ability breadth:** standardized points in the second-best group. **Outcomes:** switching (H2), Δ (H3), active senior (≥2 approved results in a calendar year at age ≥20) and senior level (best standardized points at 18–20 among athletes active then) for H2–H4. **Covariates:** sex, birth cohort, main-group level at 13–14, best-group level, competition days at 13–14 and 15–16, k13, k15, log club size, club clustering; birth quarter where available.

## 4. Analysis plan

H1: shares with Wilson CIs; distribution of the match gap. H2a: logistic regression of switching on mismatch with covariates; cluster-robust SE by club as sensitivity. H2b: among switchers, observed share switching to the best 13–14 group minus the chance-expected share (per athlete, 1 over the number of other groups competed in), bootstrap CI; repeated for switches by 17–19. H3: linear regression of standardized Δ on switch group (towards best; mismatched non-switcher; matched non-switcher; other switch) with covariates, contrasts with CIs; repeated with best- and second-best-group level as covariates. H4: logistic (active senior) and linear (senior level) regressions in the ≥2-group subsample, with and without ability breadth and best-group level; bootstrap CI for the change in the breadth coefficient. Secondary: senior activity and level by match and switch group; landmark Cox model as in A1. Replication in both cohort halves.

**Robustness (pre-specified).** Regression to the mean: best group defined on ≥3 results; negative control with the "best group" drawn at random among the athlete's other groups. Group definitions: four groups (hurdles merged with sprint); runs split into middle and long distance. Match threshold 0.5 SD. Mean-of-3-best vs. maximum. Wind-legal marks only. COVID-19 (born 2004–2005): cohort dummies, exclusion, interaction. Birth quarter: H2 and H3 stratified Q1–Q2 vs. Q3–Q4 (descriptive). k ≥ 2 in both windows.

**Inference.** Two-sided α = 0.05 for H2a and H3; H2b and H4 reported with CIs whatever the result; the robustness set is interpreted as a pattern, not as independent tests.

**Power (approximate).** With about 4 900 landmark athletes, about 29 % mismatched and a switching share of the order of one third, H2a has good precision for ORs down to about 1.2; H3 has about 1 400 mismatched athletes to split by switching, so a 0.2 SD difference in Δ is detectable; H4 is a subsample analysis treated as a directional test.

## 5. Ethics and transparency

Competition results registered by the federation; no performance analysis below age 13; no individuals identified; switching and match never characterized at the individual level. Same legal basis as the earlier registry studies (outside the Health Research Act; Sikt assessment obtained). Code is version-controlled; athlete-level files stay outside the repository and are regenerable from the registry. Deviations from this plan will be reported as such.
